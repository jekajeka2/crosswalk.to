# PitchBook Research (Oct. 17): 1) Continuation funds are becoming a way to hold winners: CV-related exits rose from under 1% of global PE exit value to a 7.3% peak in 2025; ~80% of CV assets entered within seven years of the first investment; IT single-asset CVs doubled from 5 to 10 (2023-25), value $1.9B to $7.3B; "CV-squared" rollovers went from 1 in 2024 to 6 in 2025, plus 2 in 2026. 2) European take-privates are in their second-strongest year on record, UK ~60% of value, driven by bigger funds and a shrinking listed universe (European IPOs down 80%+ from 2021). 3) PitchBook data: US VC put $98B+ into 3,640 companies in Q3; US space and defense startups raised $32.6B YTD vs $16.8B in all of 2025; robotics companies raised $48B YTD; Seattle-area startups raised $3.5B in Q3, the most in any quarter in at least a decade; only 18 companies went public in Q3, leaving 992 active unicorns.

Continuation funds (Jinny Choi, senior research analyst, PE): IT's share rose because sponsors that bought software at peak multiples won't sell at today's marks and AI-disruption fears have compressed valuations, so GPs buy time; CV-squared is the stress test of whether CVs are a durable liquidity tool or a way to defer exits indefinitely. Analyst note: "The Continuation of Continuation Funds"
Context: PitchBook's Q3 US PE Breakdown counted 95 US CV deals worth $47.4B YTD vs 161 and $97.6B in all of 2025
European take-privates (Nicolas Moura, senior research analyst, EMEA private capital): unlike 2022's rate-driven repricing, 2026 deal value nears the all-time record while public markets rise; Europe's largest funds grew 50%+, raising check sizes; LSE delistings outpace new listings. Several targets were performing well and went private over listing costs, not distress. Analyst note: "Private by Choice: Europe's Rise in PE Take-Privates"
