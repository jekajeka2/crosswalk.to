# Newcomer: AI is stretching the VC megafund model to its limits. To write Anthropic-sized checks, Menlo, Lightspeed and ICONIQ raised new funds, pushed LP co-invests and marketed SPVs, but the model depends on a small pool of giant LPs who have seen little cash back in a decade, while unicorns lack exits. Separately, the Strebulaev/Jackson ranking of 50 AI-focused VCs puts a16z first, then Lightspeed, Menlo, Sequoia and ICONIQ Growth.

Thesis: the barbell is widening. Megafunds dominate (Anthropic's S-1 cap table is expected to be mostly corporates and late-stage megafunds), while wealthy non-traditional investors write outsized checks: Sound Ventures put $35M and Factorial Funds $50M into Anthropic's Series C after Sand Hill giants passed; Valor Equity Partners won big on SpaceX
Risk: M&A is weak on high rates, IPO windows only open for the best, and the late-week AI stock rout narrows them further; LPs sit on paper gains with little liquidity to re-up into ever-larger funds
Takeaway: mid-tier firms and emerging managers are often locked out or pay inflated prices in multi-valuation rounds, so Newcomer argues the opportunity lies in going against the herd
Ranking: Stanford GSB's Ilya Strebulaev and Ohio State's Blake Jackson rank 50 AI-focused VCs on valuations, growth rates, dilution, capital distributions and value-add; Menlo Ventures and Dragoneer scored well thanks to heavy AI concentration and deals like Anthropic
