# a16z, leading Eka Robotics' Series A: dexterous manipulation is one of robotics' biggest openings, and vision and language models alone can't do it, because contact needs touch. Eka pairs a Vision-Force-Action model (vision plus force feedback from its hands) with reinforcement learning in physics simulation, so robot experience can scale with compute rather than human demonstrations, provided the sim-to-real gap keeps closing.

Thesis: much of the physical economy (manufacturing, electronics assembly, logistics, food production) still depends on human hands; industrial robots only handle fixtured, repetitive work
Argument: knowing what to do (vision, language, world models) is different from doing it; once contact begins (e.g. inserting a connector), force and slip feedback matter more than vision
Eka's approach: model, sensing, controller and hardware designed together so contact itself becomes information; policies trained by RL in simulation over millions of variations, including failure modes humans rarely demonstrate
Risk the essay names: contact dynamics are hard to simulate, and small errors in friction or actuator modeling can break real-world transfer
Founders cited: Pulkit Agrawal (MIT CSAIL Improbable AI Lab) and Tuomas Haarnoja (co-creator of Soft Actor-Critic)
Round (per Axios Pro Rata): $150M Series A at a $1.45B valuation, co-led by a16z and F-Prime, with Obvious Ventures and E14 Fund
Reported by: a16z, Axios Pro Rata
