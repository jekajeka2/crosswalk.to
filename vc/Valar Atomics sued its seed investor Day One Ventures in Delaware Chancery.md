# Valar Atomics sued its seed investor Day One Ventures in Delaware Chancery Court for trying to enforce pro rata rights on Valar's Sequoia-led Series B at a $6B valuation, and CEO Isaiah Taylor took the fight public on social media. Axios' Dan Primack: pro rata is in most early-stage deals and in NVCA model documents, and killing it may cost founders by shrinking the number of early-stage funds.

Day One (founder Masha Bucher) invested in Valar's seed, Series A and Series A extension, and responded publicly.
Supporters of pro rata: early investors take outsized risk, the right is priced into the deal, and doubling down on winners often returns whole funds.
Opponents: it stirs conflict; useful investors get invited back anyway, and sometimes the company needs a deeper or more strategic investor.
New pressure: growth-stage firms that need to deploy billions push founders on pro rata, including through rounds tranched by valuation.
In practice the contract terms are a starting point for negotiation; neither side wants a reputation for breaching contracts or playing hardball.
