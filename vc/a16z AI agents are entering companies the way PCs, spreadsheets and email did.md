# a16z: AI agents are entering companies the way PCs, spreadsheets and email did, chaotically and bottom-up, and the winners will "let chaos reign, then rein in chaos" (Andy Grove): a governed habitat that keeps the bottom-up adoption alive instead of shutting it down. Written for the launch of portfolio company Replit's "Agent Habitat" (Replit for Business).

Thesis: a chaotic entrance is one of the best signs a technology will change work. Today agents run in chat windows, personal accounts and one-off scripts with whatever credentials someone handed them, and nobody decided what they can touch.
Historical parallels: 1980s employees disguised PC purchases on procurement forms ("shadow" data processing); "rogue spreadsheets" were the original hallucinating workplace agent, later tamed by data-access rules, locked cells, version history and audits.
Implication: the opportunity is the governance layer (permissions, visibility, audit) that lets bottom-up agent use scale inside companies.
Disclosure: Replit is an a16z portfolio company; the post accompanies its product launch.
