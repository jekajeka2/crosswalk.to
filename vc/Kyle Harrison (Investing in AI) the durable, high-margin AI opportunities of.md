# Kyle Harrison (Investing in AI): the durable, high-margin AI opportunities of the next decade sit in what keeps GPUs running, not in models or chips, because constraints migrate. Of ~120 GW of data center capacity scheduled through Aug 2028, historically only about half lands on time, held up by transformers, gas turbines and 24-48-month utility interconnection queues. He names four next bottlenecks: power, cooling (direct-to-chip and immersion), optical networking, and inference moving to the edge.

Framework: Howard Marks-style second-order thinking. The first-order winner sells the new thing; second-order winners sell what it can't live without (cars led to highways, suburbs and McDonald's; mobile led to Uber and Instagram)
First-order AI trade (Nvidia, TSMC, OpenAI, Anthropic, hyperscalers) is priced for perfection
Constraint migration: relieve one bottleneck and the economics move to the next one
1. Gigawatt bottleneck: per-rack power draw forces bespoke generation (SMRs, gas, geothermal) and upgrades to transmission, transformers and switchgear; cites Flex CEO Revathi Advaithi: AI is "a power story"
2. Thermal wall: next-gen racks outrun air cooling; liquid cooling moves the heat out of the rack to the roof and the water supply
3. Speed of light: tying 100,000+ GPUs together needs optical transceivers, silicon photonics and InfiniBand/Ethernet switching at unprecedented scale
4. Edge migration: latency, bandwidth and cost push inference to phones, PCs, cars and machines, demand that never touches the data center
"Boring bull market": HVAC and industrial cooling, power and uranium, fiber and optical components become stealth AI plays
Venture angle: cooling fluids and heat exchangers, SMRs and fusion, photonics startups; long-dated and capital intensive
Reported by: Investing in AI (Kyle Harrison)
