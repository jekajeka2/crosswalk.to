# Clouded Judgement (Jamin Ball): the "diffusion gap" between what AI models can do and how average users use them is the crux of whether revenue catches up with AI capex. Only ~2% of US households pay for any AI service (PNC research), while power users' spend keeps climbing with model progress. He bets the gap closes on an S-curve rather than slowly, because easier tools (personal agents like Muse/Instinct, which made OpenClaw-style agents usable) let unsophisticated users adopt, as browsers did for the internet.

Thesis: markets questioning whether AI revenue will match capex commitments (semi-cap selloff, CoreWeave solvency chatter) hinge on average users, not power users, starting to spend more.
Data: PNC research shows ~2% of US households pay for an AI service; OpenAI usage data shows power and median users' usage inflecting while the average user lags.
Two paths: slow diffusion, or a typical S-curve adoption across consumers and businesses; Ball expects something closer to the S-curve.
Mechanism: past diffusion gaps closed because tools abstracted complexity (browsers for the internet, Google Maps for GPS), not because users got more sophisticated; OpenClaw stayed with power users until personal agents (Muse, Instinct) wrapped it for mainstream use.
Prompted by an Andrej Karpathy post arguing AI diffusion is happening more slowly.
