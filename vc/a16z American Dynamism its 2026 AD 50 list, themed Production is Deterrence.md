# a16z American Dynamism: its 2026 AD 50 list, themed "Production is Deterrence," argues US deterrence now depends on manufacturing capacity, so defense founders must design for mass production from day one and onshore the full chain. The 50 companies are grouped into Systems (e.g. Anduril, Apex, Astranis), Factories (e.g. Amca, Arda) and Inputs (e.g. Ambrosia Energy).

Thesis: defense startups are moving from prototypes to full-scale production, and the industrial base (factories, suppliers, critical materials, energy) must keep pace; cites Ukraine-driven NATO restocking, US munitions gaps exposed by the Iran conflict, and China's rare earth export curbs
Three pillars: Systems deliver capability; Factories provide plants, tools and software to build at volume; Inputs supply materials and power
Examples from the list: Anduril (Fury CCA production contracts in June 2026; first Ohio-built Fury off Arsenal-1; $20B-ceiling Army enterprise contract); Apex (standard satellite buses, targeting 200+ satellites a year at Factory One in LA); Arda (Bob McGrew's hardware assembly-planning software, launched October 2026 with $230M total funding, used in Anduril's ArsenalOS); Ambrosia Energy (solar-plus-battery plants for off-grid businesses at about $100/MWh, building in West Texas); Amca (re-engineered defense and aerospace components); Astranis (MicroGEO satellites, five in orbit and five in production as of May 2026)
