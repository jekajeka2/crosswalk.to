# AENU and Currence compared the founders of Europe's 100 best-funded climate companies (each raised €50M+) with 100 US founders. Europe has no "founder campus": its 100 founders came from 118 schools, and Cambridge produced the most (5), against 30 from Harvard, Stanford and MIT in the US. European climate funds raised $61B in 2025 vs. $37B in the US, with a 71% fund close rate vs. 35%, but founders still look to the US for scale-up capital.

Degrees: 55 Europeans stopped at a master's vs. 18 Americans; 28 Americans hold an MBA vs. 11 Europeans
Experience: median of 16 years of work at founding in the US vs. 13 in Europe; ages nearly identical (about 40% founded before 35, a third at 35-44, about a quarter at 45+)
Serial founders: 63 US vs. 51 Europe; at least 25 Europeans had already sold or taken public an earlier company
Pipeline through incumbents: about a third spent most of their careers in industry or energy (32 EU, 31 US); 72 Europeans and 66 Americans worked at a tier-1 employer such as Siemens, Shell or McKinsey
Concentration: batteries, solar and low-carbon materials took 58% of the roughly €80B raised by European climate companies
Growth capital: many European companies lean on debt instead of equity. Elvy raised a ~$585M debt facility against under $12M of equity; Cloover paired a $22M Series A with $1.3B+ in EIF-guaranteed debt
CTVC/Elemental Impact 2026 founder survey: 73% of founders named capital structure a top financing challenge; 47% said non-dilutive grants would speed their growth; 29% pointed to blended finance
Reported by: CTVC
