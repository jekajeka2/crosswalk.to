# a16z, "Wallet Wars": now that personal agents (Instinct, OpenAI's Dots, Meta's Muse) can act, the fight is over who gives them permission to pay. Existing wallets have the head start: PayPal 400M+ users, Stripe Link 300M, Shop Pay 250M, with Stripe and Shopify already powering Muse and Instinct. The bigger battle is whether an agent pays as you or through a middleman. Link's single-use virtual cards reach any merchant but split each purchase into two transactions, which breaks merchant loyalty offers.

Two separate contests: who helps the agent pay, and who lets it transact as the user. The essay argues the second matters more, for personal payment flows like loyalty programs and for payments economics.
Agents build on decades of payment tokenization, which separates the paying account's identity from the authority to start a payment.
Example: an agent buys at Walmart with a Link virtual card, Walmart offers Walmart+, and the signup fails because the cards don't match.
Thesis: Stripe is building the "AOL of agentic commerce," a walled garden where purchases at Stripe merchants never exchange credentials, with Link virtual cards covering everyone else.
PayPal's existing consumer and merchant network could give it a second act.
