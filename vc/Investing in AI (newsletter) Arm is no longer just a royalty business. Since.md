# Investing in AI (newsletter): Arm is no longer just a royalty business. Since March 2026 it has sold its own finished silicon (the AGI CPU), which could grow its addressable revenue roughly tenfold but puts it in competition with the chipmakers that pay it royalties. Management's FY31 plan targets $10B of IP/CSS revenue at >65% operating margin plus $15B of AGI CPU revenue at >30%, for >$9 non-GAAP EPS. At a ~$295B market cap, the essay argues, much of that plan is already priced in.

Q1 FY27: record $1.29B revenue (+22% YoY); royalties $715M (+22%); data-center royalties more than doubled.
Royalty growth slowing: Q2 guided to low teens, full year to high teens, as smartphone weakness and memory costs are partly offset by a richer Armv9/CSS mix.
AGI CPU: Arm cites >$2B of customer demand across FY27-FY28. The essay reads that as pipeline, not contracted backlog. Manufacturing capacity is secured only for the earlier $1B opportunity.
Price as of 8 Oct 2026: $275.29, ~1.07B diluted shares; well below the 52-week high.
Agentic AI could add CPU compute around each accelerator cluster, a tailwind for Arm's data-center sockets.
Open question: whether a large merchant-silicon business weakens the IP platform economics (channel conflict with AWS, Google, Microsoft, Nvidia, Qualcomm and MediaTek).
What to watch: the Qualcomm contract trial in Delaware, Q2 FY27 results on 4 Nov, AGI CPU demand turning into shipments, and whether related-party licensing (SoftBank affiliates, Arm China) becomes durable third-party royalties.
The full analysis is paywalled; these points come from the free executive summary. The author holds no ARM position.
