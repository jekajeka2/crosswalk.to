# PitchBook Research (Oct. 24): 1) PitchBook raised its Polymarket valuation estimate to $20.6B from $12B: US trading volume rose 140% MoM in the 30 days to Oct. 14 (sports combos and crypto event contracts), international fell 4.3%, and Polymarket cut US trading rebates on Oct. 19 to keep more fees. PitchBook still prefers Kalshi for its Robinhood/Webull distribution and higher margins; a possible Supreme Court ruling in 2027 is the key risk for both. 2) Fintech VC in Q3: $10.8B across 536 deals, slightly down QoQ but in line with the quarterly average since 2022; exits soft, and Stripe's $8B purchase of OpenRouter is read as a sign that incumbents will buy more AI-native startups. 3) PitchBook data: the Bay Area raised $352B for VC funds from 2020 through 2025, ~46% of all US VC; neolabs have raised a combined $23B; 2026 is on track to be a record for both the count and value of first-time financings; gaming VC is $13.7B across 430 deals YTD.

Polymarket: PitchBook estimate $20.6B, up from $12B at initiation of coverage (Franco Granda, senior research analyst)
Polymarket US trading volume +140% MoM in the 30 days through Oct. 14; international volume -4.3%
Polymarket cut US trading rebates on Oct. 19; PitchBook's higher revenue forecast assumes traders stay despite thinner incentives
PitchBook still favors Kalshi: brokerage integrations (Robinhood, Webull) give lower acquisition cost and higher margins
Risk: market access is contested in court; a possible US Supreme Court intervention is expected to shape the opportunity in 2027
Q3 2026 fintech VC: $10.8B across 536 deals, slightly down from Q2, in line with the quarterly average since 2022
Fintech exits remained soft; broader M&A active, including Stripe's $8B acquisition of OpenRouter
Bay Area VC fundraising 2020 through 2025: $352B, about 46% of all US venture capital (cited by TechCrunch)
Neolabs have raised a combined $23B (cited by The Economist)
2026 is set to be a record year for both the count and value of first-time financings (cited by WSJ)
Gaming VC deal value YTD: $13.7B across 430 deals (cited by Fortune)
AI buildout note (Jim Corridore, lead analyst, industrials): construction and engineering firms tied to data centers draw more PE dollars than any other sector PitchBook tracks, chips included; Nvidia invested $2B each in optical suppliers Lumentum and Coherent; VoltaGrid runs 1.4 GW of behind-the-meter gas microgrids for customers including Oracle Cloud Infrastructure
