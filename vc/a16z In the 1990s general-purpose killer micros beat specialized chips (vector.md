# a16z: In the 1990s general-purpose "killer micros" beat specialized chips (vector, MPP, Lisp machines) even on the specialists' own workloads, through a cycle of generality driving volume, volume funding performance, and absorbing the specialists' best ideas (vector units became SSE/AVX). AI workloads have reopened the debate, with an explosion of accelerators and GPUs now out-earning CPUs, under constraints that differ from last time.

Casualties of the last boom: ETA Systems (1989), Multiflow (1990), Alliant (1992), Symbolics (1993), Kendall Square Research and Thinking Machines (1994), Cray Computer (1995).
Hennessy and Patterson: single-processor performance grew 50%+ a year from 1986 to 2003, mostly from clock speed.
Example: DVD video decoder cards died within about two years once the Pentium II could decode in software.
The open question the essay poses: do today's AI accelerators hold, or get absorbed into general-purpose chips again?
