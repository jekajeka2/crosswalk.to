# a16z: American manufacturing is an investable asset class. Defense-tech startups (Castelion, Saronic, Anduril and others) are moving from prototypes to production. The bottleneck is now a fragmented, tiny-firm supplier base that VC has largely ignored: of 16,876 US machine shops, 83% employ fewer than 20 people.

- Thesis: raising capital and winning development contracts matter less than production and fielding at scale. The industrial base under new defense primes can't keep pace, and Silicon Valley hasn't funded the manufacturers and supply chain beneath them.
- Data: the 2022 Economic Census counted 16,876 machine shops. Among those operating all year, 83% employed fewer than 20 people and 95% fewer than 50. About three-quarters of the 240,644 US manufacturing employers have fewer than 20 employees.
- Constraint: besides size, the base is fragmented across specialized shops that rarely work as a coordinated network, and some critical processes have only one or two qualified sources.
- Example: Castelion's Blackbeard hypersonic missile is going through the prototype-to-production transition, with a Navy production order for hundreds of missiles a year and a path to thousands.
- Why now: US policy to cut reliance on China for batteries, motors, actuators, turbines and rare earths. The essay argues America should compete with capital and technology, not labor cost, and build a technology-first supplier hierarchy from primes down to precision machine shops.
