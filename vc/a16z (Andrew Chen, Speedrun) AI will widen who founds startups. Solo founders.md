# a16z (Andrew Chen, Speedrun): AI will widen who founds startups. Solo founders, non-technical founders, domain experts, teenagers and content creators all become viable. Each archetype has a counter-case: a demo is not a reliable product, insiders rarely reinvent their own industries, and an audience is not a growth engine. Expect high failure rates but a bigger founder tent.

Author: Andrew Chen, a16z general partner on Speedrun (first checks up to $1M into new startups)
Solo founders: AI may let one person do what used to take business plus technical cofounders. Counterpoint (Ben Horowitz): the bar for a seed-stage v1 rises too, so an AI-multiplied team may still beat a solopreneur, and coding may never have been the main bottleneck
Non-technical founders (finance, consulting, creatives) can now build. Counterpoint: technical judgment still matters for scaling past a demo, especially in dev tools
Domain experts (doctors, brokers, teachers) can solve their own problems. Counterpoint: hoteliers didn't create Airbnb and finance didn't create Kalshi
Kids and students start earlier with AI. Counterpoint: they lack hiring, fundraising and domain experience outside consumer
Content creators bring built-in distribution. Counterpoint: one spike of reach is not a sustainable growth engine
History: each wave crowns a "yes" archetype: Stanford PhDs after Google, dropouts in the Facebook era, design-led founders with Airbnb, operators with Uber, and maybe CS PhDs again for AI infra
Reported by: a16z
