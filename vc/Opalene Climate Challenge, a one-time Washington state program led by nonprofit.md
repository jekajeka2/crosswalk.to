# Opalene Climate Challenge, a one-time Washington state program led by nonprofit VertueLab, made recoverable grants to six early-stage climate hardware startups picked from ~40 applicants: $100K each to Airbuild, Ocean and ZILA BioWorks, $25K each to Azotera, Climate Solutions International and Emerald Battery Labs. Funded solely by philanthropic donor-advised funds after a planned state match proved too slow, as AI/data-center deals drain climate VC.

- Structure: recoverable grants from donor-advised funds; successful investments return proceeds to the donor's fund or another designated nonprofit.
- Original plan was matching dollars from Washington state; organizers dropped it because raising that capital would take too long.
- $100K recipients: Airbuild (microalgae turning wastewater treatment plants into fertilizer factories; also won the $5K Audience Choice Award), Ocean (low-cost, low-carbon bamboo roofing/building panels), ZILA BioWorks (plant-based resins and epoxies).
- $25K recipients: Azotera (low-cost ammonia for energy storage and farm use), Climate Solutions International (software for government infrastructure analysis: resilience, cost, carbon), Emerald Battery Labs (sodium-ion batteries replacing lead-acid in commercial fleets and data centers).
- Five of the six are commercializing University of Washington or Washington State University technology; several went through Cascadia Clean Tech Accelerator, UW CoMotion Labs Climate Tech Incubator, or the E8 angel group.
- Rationale per co-director Allison Arnold: climate startups not aligned with data-center demand face a capital pinch, and the Pacific Northwest lacks the risk capital of Silicon Valley, Boston or New York.
- Winners were featured at the Seattle Climate Innovation Hub.
