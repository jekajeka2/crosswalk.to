# a16z: Databricks now gets more queries from AI agents than from people, and agents launch 17M+ databases on it every day, up from every interaction involving a human three years ago. Databricks has passed a $7B run rate growing 80% y/y. a16z argues agents need a new kind of system of record, and that as agents outnumber people the job of most workers shifts toward "everyone is a manager", undoing Burnham's managerial-revolution model of centralized decision-making.

Thesis: what employees do all day is constrained by their employer's system of record and decision-making, which is itself constrained by database technology
Two shifts: agentic automation of existing tasks, and frontline employees investigating and acting without waiting on hierarchy
Databricks as four stacked S-curves: Spark, Lakehouse, Lakebase, agents
Databricks metrics cited: $7B+ run rate, 80% y/y growth; more agent queries than human; 17M+ databases launched by agents per day
