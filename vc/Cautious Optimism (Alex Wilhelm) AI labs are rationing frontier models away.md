# Cautious Optimism (Alex Wilhelm): AI labs are rationing frontier models away from free users, opening an intelligence gap by ability to pay. From Oct 9, Google limits free Gemini users to 3.5 Flash-Lite, dropping 3.6 Flash and 3.1 Pro; OpenAI and Anthropic have made similar moves.

Thesis: as compute costs bite, labs push their free and lowest-paying users onto smaller models, so the best AI goes to whoever pays. Wilhelm calls this a form of 'permanent underclass'.
Evidence: Google's updated support doc says Gemini app users without a Google AI subscription will be limited to 3.5 Flash-Lite starting Oct 9, after a compute-based usage change in May (via 9to5Google). OpenAI steers lower-priced tiers to its small Luna model, and Anthropic made Fable less accessible after its promotional period.
Also in the issue: prediction markets put Democrats at 91-93% to take the House and 65-66% for the Senate (Kalshi, Polymarket), against tech-right voices like Keith Rabois and Chamath calling a GOP win. Aleph Alpha (now merged with Cohere) released Kolibri, an open German/English model, and Nvidia-backed Reflection AI is reportedly preparing an open-weight model.
