# MGV's Marc Schröder: AI megarounds hide a shrinking Series A market, so seed founders should size the seed to reach Series A milestones. U.S. Series A count fell 28% year over year in Q3 (272 vs 377). Only 24% of 2023 $1M+ seed companies have raised past seed, and 16% of the 2024 cohort, down from 55%+ through 2020.

Megarounds: at least 114 Series A rounds of $100M+ globally this year, more than 70% to AI companies
Q3 U.S. counts: 272 Series A rounds vs 377 a year earlier; seed held up better at 984 vs 1,059
Seed size: the median U.S. seed was about $3M last year, 3x its 2018 level
Graduation: 24% of 2023 $1M+ seed companies have moved past seed, 16% of the 2024 cohort, down from 55%+ through 2020
The bar: Uncork's Andy McLoughlin says Series A investors now expect $2M-$3M ARR (sometimes $4M), and seed-to-A now takes more than two years
Advice: raise a seed that reaches that bar with six months to spare; find out which $100M-$500M funds lead at your stage and send their partners updates for a year before asking; a repeatable sales process at $2M ARR beats founder-led sales at $3M
Example: Atomic (NYC supply chain software) raised a $3M seed in April 2025, then a $12.5M Series A about 17 months later
Note: the author runs MGV, which sells a go-to-market program to its founders
