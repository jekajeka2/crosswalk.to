# Benchmark's partners say AI is breaking VC's rule against backing direct rivals: as building software gets nearly free, founders try to claim every adjacent market, so portfolio companies run into each other. Exclusivity still matters at the very earliest stage, Eric Vishria said, but Peter Fenton called conflicts the firm's toughest problem ("kryptonite").

Setting: Benchmark partners Eric Vishria, Chetan Puttagunta and Peter Fenton, on stage at TechCrunch Disrupt this week
Vishria: every company changes fast, so "you are going to get conflicts"; Benchmark keeps what it learns confidential, and "we are not product or technology experts"
Puttagunta: backing a direct competitor from day one is "an easy answer" (no), but a portfolio company expanding into another's turf happens, and AI will make it more common
Fenton: Benchmark sits closer to a co-founder than to a stock picker, and its rule is transparency: no surprises after the fact
Context: OpenAI and Anthropic now share dozens of investors; Factory fired board observer Chris Degnan over alleged conduct involving rival Cognition, which he then joined as CRO; Khosla Ventures backs both Factory and Cognition
Reported by: TechCrunch (Connie Loizos), also in StrictlyVC
