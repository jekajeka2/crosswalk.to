# a16z: frontier models can now generate CAD, design PCBs and source off-the-shelf parts, but superhuman hardware engineers will be gated by the lossy digital-physical interface: hard to "write" designs into atoms (e.g. CAD-to-CAM translation for CNC parts, procurement) and hard to "read" physical measurements back in, so even models that one-shot designs will have much more work to do.

Evidence: hardware benchmarks (CAD Arena, EE Bench, the author's LuxoBench) show steady gains, but in LuxoBench tests of GPT-6 Astra, Fable 5.1 and Grok 4.6 building an interactive desk lamp, the models alone were "not quite ready for primetime hardware engineering"
Framework: 101 level = decompose a product into compatible COTS parts and procure them; 201 level = custom parts, where a machinist must translate CAD into CAM instructions for the machine
