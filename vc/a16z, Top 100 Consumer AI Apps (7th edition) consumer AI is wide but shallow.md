# a16z, Top 100 Consumer AI Apps (7th edition): consumer AI is wide but shallow. About half of US consumers use AI, 25% daily, and just 4.5% held a paid ChatGPT, Gemini or Claude subscription in August. The top 10% of spenders make up about half of observed spending, mostly on coding, productivity and creative tools.

- Only 11 first-time entrants, the fewest across the seven editions
- New this edition: a ranking by observed US consumer card spend (YipitData), which captures desktop apps and agents inside messaging platforms
- Thesis: nearly all consumer AI revenue is subscriptions or usage fees, with no ads or transaction revenue. a16z argues this limits deeper adoption and that consumer AI likely needs a new (or old, ad/transactional) business model
- Sees early signs of free personal assistants usable by non-developers
- Method: top 50 web products by monthly visits (Similarweb) and top 50 mobile apps by MAU (Sensor Tower); NSFW-focused products now excluded
