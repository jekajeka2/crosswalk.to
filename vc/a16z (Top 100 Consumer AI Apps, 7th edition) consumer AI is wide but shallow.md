# a16z (Top 100 Consumer AI Apps, 7th edition): consumer AI is wide but shallow. Nearly half of U.S. consumers report using AI, but only 25% use it daily and just 4.5% had an active paid personal ChatGPT, Gemini or Claude subscription as of August. The top 10% of spenders account for about half of observed spending, mostly on coding, productivity and creative tools.

- New this edition: a ranking by observed spending on U.S. consumer cards (YipitData), alongside the usual traffic rankings (top 50 web by Similarweb visits, top 50 mobile by Sensor Tower MAU)
- Only 11 first-time entrants, the fewest debuts across the seven lists
- Thesis: consumer AI's first real market is power users paying for tools that help them build, create and get work done; reaching everyone else will take different products (free personal assistants usable by non-developers) and likely a different business model, since almost all AI revenue so far comes from subscriptions or usage charges rather than ads or transactions
- Eligibility change: products designed mainly for NSFW use are now excluded and treated as a separate category
