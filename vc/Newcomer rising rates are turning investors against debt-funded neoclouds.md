# Newcomer: rising rates are turning investors against debt-funded neoclouds. Firmus pulled its IPO at a $30B target on $59M FY2026 revenue and 46MW delivered of a 912MW pipeline; Nscale filed at $35B on similar promised-vs-online math; CoreWeave bonds still yield ~15% when its model needs ~6% investment-grade money, and its founders have sold $3.3B of stock since IPO.

Thesis: the most levered AI-infrastructure bets (neoclouds financed by Nvidia equity and private credit from Apollo, Blackstone) face "carnage to come" as rates rise and AI demand signals soften.
Firmus Grid (Australia; backed by Nvidia, Coatue, Jane Street, Blackstone): IPO pulled after investors balked at a $30B valuation; says it will still list in New York next year and raise private funds meanwhile
Nscale: filed to go public last month at a $35B valuation built on Microsoft and Anthropic contracts; Newcomer calls the deal already shaky
CoreWeave: debt traded at distress levels mid-week; shares down 30% over recent weeks but still ~60% above the IPO price; CEO Mike Intrator blamed "irrational markets"; Jensen Huang said its cost of capital is falling
Anthropic Q3 revenue near $17B, up 46% QoQ vs ~100% the prior quarter; 5-7% of revenue from distillation per the FT; projects $500B+ of data-center spend over the next decade
Also in the issue: Ramp data shows enterprise AI token spend leveling out; Bumble taken private by Ithaca Holdings, a buyout vehicle for top VCs
Reported by: Newcomer
