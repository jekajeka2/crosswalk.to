# Space discoveries Feb–Aug 2026: first confirmed atmosphere on a habitable-zone rocky exoplanet (LHS 1140 b, via escaping helium); TOI-561 b unexpectedly holds a thick atmosphere; methane on temperate exo-Saturn TOI-199 b; Beta Pictoris d found; a "black hole star" (young BH wrapped in dense gas) proposed as the engine of Webb's little red dots; a galaxy 1.3 Gyr after the Big Bang with three black holes, two about to merge; dust and water surviving near Sgr A*; Milky Way gamma-ray excess revived as a dark matter candidate; ALMA study says SETI has ignored higher radio frequencies; Rubin LSST began June 30, 2026.

# Notable space findings, Feb–Aug 2026

## Exoplanets
- **LHS 1140 b** — first confirmed atmosphere on a rocky planet in another star's habitable zone, detected via helium slowly escaping (July 2026). Arguably the headline result of the period.
- **TOI-561 b** — a scorching, fast-orbiting rocky planet once thought too extreme to keep any gas turned out to have a thick atmosphere (Webb, March 2026).
- **TOI-199 b** — a rare giant planet with near-Earth temperatures; Webb detected methane (Astronomical Journal 171:354, May 2026).
- A giant planet ~700 ly away shows a daily weather cycle: mineral clouds form every morning and vanish by night (Webb, May 2026).
- **Beta Pictoris d** — a second giant planet found hiding in the nearby Beta Pictoris system (Webb, April 2026).

## Black holes and the early universe
- **"Black hole star"** — a rapidly growing early black hole wrapped in dense gas, described in Nature (Aug 17, 2026, ISTA-led). Proposed as the power source of Webb's "little red dots," and a route to billion-solar-mass black holes soon after the Big Bang.
- A galaxy just 1.3 billion years after the Big Bang hosts **three black holes**; two near the center will merge soon. Evidence that mergers were a major contributor to SMBH growth (Webb, Aug 2026).
- A black hole shredded a massive star in one of the most energetic stellar explosions ever observed; something may have survived the event (Aug 2026).
- **Dust and water survive near Sgr A*** — the evolved star IRS 3 keeps enriching its surroundings despite the radiation environment (Webb, Aug 11, 2026).

## Dark matter
- The Milky Way's central **gamma-ray excess**, long debated as millisecond pulsars vs. dark matter annihilation, got a new study (June 30, 2026) favoring dark matter.

## SETI
- Using archived ALMA data, researchers argued technosignature searches have covered only a narrow radio slice and largely ignored higher frequencies (July 30, 2026).

## Coming soon
- **Rubin Observatory** officially began the 10-year Legacy Survey of Space and Time on June 30, 2026. Expect a flood of asteroid, supernova, and transient discoveries.
- **Roman Space Telescope** is slated for launch in fall 2026.

*Compiled from ScienceDaily, Universe Today, ESA/Webb, NASA Science, and Rubin Observatory releases. Some results are preprint-stage.*
